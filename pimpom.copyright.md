Rules that matter:

It must be a person or a legal entity, not the app name. “2026 Ping me” would be wrong.
The year is when the rights were obtained — first creation or publication — not necessarily the current year. For a new app shipping now, 2026 is right. Keep it at 2026 in future updates; don’t bump it annually. Change it only if there’s a genuinely new copyrightable work.
It should match the entity on your developer account. A mismatch between a personal account and a company copyright line is the kind of thing that draws questions during review, and it muddles ownership if you later transfer the app.
Skip “All rights reserved.” The field is short and Apple’s format doesn’t use it.

Two related things:

Google Play has no copyright field. If you want the notice visible on Android, put it in an about or settings row. Same string: © 2026 Nhick Flores.

In-app notice, if you add one to the Profile screen or an about sheet:

© 2026 16bits.dev. All rights reserved.

That one can include “All rights reserved” — it’s conventional in-app, even though it carries no legal weight in most jurisdictions.

One thing worth deciding before you submit: if you’re planning to form a company for this, do it before first publication. Transferring an app between developer accounts later is possible but tedious, and the copyright line, privacy policy, and payment profile all have to move together.
